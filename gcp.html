My role

  • Founding and capital. Founded the company and secured CHF 800k+ in soft equity commitments from private investors ahead of commercial registration in Q1 2027.
  • Underwriting. Built the model from land acquisition to exit: construction budget, financing costs, operating expenses and five-year cash flows, stress-tested across sell and hold scenarios before any commitment. The base case targets a 35% investor IRR on a two-year build-and-sell exit.
  • Investor terms. Structured the investor terms and fee model, and benchmarked pricing against 100+ regional market comparables.
  • Partners and communes. Work with general contractor Group Comina on cost estimation and permitting strategy, and lead early engagement with municipal authorities.

The problem

Swiss resorts rely on thousands of seasonal workers every year, yet housing for them has become one of the scarcest assets in the Alps. Stricter building rules have raised construction costs, and new resort development favours high-end second homes. Staff end up in ageing buildings, short-term arrangements or long commutes, and hotels and restaurants pay for it in recruitment difficulties, turnover and service quality.

The approach

GCP develops small residential buildings on undeveloped land in Valais, close to Verbier, Zermatt, Saas-Fee and Crans-Montana, where hospitality demand is strongest and supply most constrained. Units are compact and built for year-round living, with durable materials and efficient layouts that keep construction and operating costs down. They are leased annually to hospitality operators under master leases: operators house their teams without tying up capital, and each project earns contracted income.

Working locally

Zoning, permitting and housing priorities change from one commune to the next, so projects are built with the community rather than around it. Communes are approached early, each building is aligned with local policy on housing for residents and workers, and GCP stays open to working with public housing bodies where it serves the building's long-term use.

Structure

Each development sits in its own dedicated vehicle, which isolates risk at project level and gives investors clear visibility on the asset they fund. Projects combine private equity with bank debt, and every site is screened for zoning compatibility, access, proximity to employers and construction feasibility before it moves forward.

The ambition: a repeatable model for Alpine worker housing, one project at a time.
